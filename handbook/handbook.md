# Example Widgets Demo Co. Employee Handbook

This handbook is fictional. It describes the policies of an invented company for a software demo and is not HR or legal guidance.

## HB-1 About this handbook

### HB-1.1 Scope

This handbook applies to every employee of Example Widgets Demo Co. from their first working day.
It covers leave, holidays, remote work, expenses, conduct, benefits, IT security, onboarding and leaving the company.
Contractors and agency workers follow the terms of their own agreements and should ask their company contact first.

### HB-1.2 How to ask a question

You can ask the HR assistant in the employee chat, ask your manager, or email the People Team at people@widgets-demo.example.
Answers from the HR assistant quote the handbook section they come from.
Questions about personal or sensitive matters are passed to the People Team, who reply directly.

### HB-1.3 Section IDs

Every section has a stable ID such as HB-4.2.
Section IDs are never renumbered or reused.
When a section is removed, its heading stays in place and is marked Retired.

### HB-1.4 When the handbook and a policy differ

The published policy rules used by the leave system take precedence over the wording of this handbook.
If you notice a difference between this handbook and a policy, tell the People Team so the handbook can be corrected.

## HB-2 Leave

### HB-2.1 Annual leave

Every employee receives 20 working days of annual leave per leave year.
The leave year runs from 1 April to 31 March.
Annual leave needs at least 5 working days of notice.
Your manager decides on annual leave requests of up to 10 consecutive working days.

### HB-2.2 Sick leave

Every employee receives 10 working days of sick leave per leave year.
Sick leave needs no advance notice.
Tell your manager that you are unwell as early as you can on the first day of absence.
If you are off sick for more than 3 consecutive working days, send a medical note to the People Team, not to your manager.

### HB-2.3 Casual leave

Every employee receives 6 working days of casual leave per leave year.
Casual leave is for short personal matters such as a home repair, a move or a family appointment.
Casual leave needs at least 1 working day of notice.

### HB-2.4 Unpaid leave

Unpaid leave has no fixed allowance.
Unpaid leave needs at least 10 working days of notice.
Every unpaid leave request must include a reason.
Every unpaid leave request is reviewed by HR.

### HB-2.5 Bereavement leave

Every employee receives 5 working days of bereavement leave per leave year.
Bereavement leave needs no advance notice.
Every bereavement leave request is reviewed by HR, who will contact you directly and can discuss extra time or support.
Individual circumstances are handled by HR in confidence.

### HB-2.6 Parental leave

Every employee receives 60 working days of parental leave per leave year.
Parental leave covers birth, adoption and becoming a parent through surrogacy or fostering.
Parental leave needs at least 20 working days of notice.
Every parental leave request is reviewed by HR.
Questions about pregnancy, health during pregnancy, or return-to-work arrangements are handled by HR in confidence.

### HB-2.7 How to request leave

Request leave through the HR assistant in the employee chat.
A leave request needs a leave type, a start date and an end date.
The HR assistant prepares your request and checks it against the leave policy, but it never approves or declines leave.
Your manager decides on annual, sick and casual leave requests of up to 10 consecutive working days.
HR reviews unpaid, bereavement and parental leave requests, and any leave request covering more than 10 consecutive working days.
If a request is missing details, you will be asked to complete it before it is sent on.

### HB-2.8 Long leave, blackout periods and team cover

A leave request covering more than 10 consecutive working days is reviewed by HR.
Weekends and public holidays inside a leave request are not counted as working days.
The year-end close blackout runs from 21 December 2026 to 31 December 2026.
Leave requests that fall inside a blackout period are flagged for review.
The team cover limit is 30 percent of a team on leave on the same working day.
Leave requests that conflict with the team cover limit are flagged for review.
Leave requests with less notice than the leave type needs are flagged for review.
Leave requests that are longer than your remaining balance are flagged for review.
The person who reviews the request, your manager or HR as set out in HB-2.7, sees each flag and decides.

### HB-2.9 Half days

You can take leave in half days.
A half day counts as 0.5 of a working day against your balance.
A half day can be at the start or the end of a leave request.

### HB-2.10 Carrying leave into the next leave year

You can carry up to 5 unused days of annual leave into the next leave year.
Carried-over annual leave must be used by 30 June of the new leave year, or it lapses.
Sick leave and casual leave cannot be carried over.
Unused bereavement and parental leave cannot be carried over.

## HB-3 Public holidays

### HB-3.1 Holiday calendar for 2026-27

The company observes six public holidays in the 2026-27 leave year.
Demo Founders Day is on Friday 1 May 2026.
Demo Summer Saturday is on Saturday 15 August 2026.
Demo Widget Day is on Friday 2 October 2026.
Demo Autumn Rest Day is on Wednesday 18 November 2026.
Demo Winter Holiday is on Friday 25 December 2026.
Demo New Year Day is on Friday 1 January 2027.

### HB-3.2 Holidays that fall on a weekend

A public holiday that falls on a Saturday or Sunday is not moved to a weekday.
No day off in lieu is given for Demo Summer Saturday.

### HB-3.3 Working on a public holiday

You work on a public holiday only when your manager has agreed it in advance.
Agree a day off in lieu with your manager for each public holiday you work, to be taken within 3 months.

## HB-4 Working hours and remote work

### HB-4.1 Core hours

Core hours are 10:00 to 16:00 in your local time zone, Monday to Friday.
Outside core hours, you can arrange your working day with your manager.
A full-time working week is 40 hours, including a daily lunch break of at least 30 minutes.

### HB-4.2 Remote-work eligibility

Employees who have completed probation can work remotely for up to 3 days per week.
Employees on probation work from the office at least 4 days per week.
Agree your regular remote days with your manager and record them in the team calendar.
Your manager can ask you to attend the office for team days, training or client visits.

### HB-4.3 Working from another country

You can work from another country for up to 10 working days per leave year if you ask HR at least 20 working days in advance.
Working from another country for longer than 10 working days needs written agreement from HR before you travel.
Working abroad can affect tax, social security and immigration status, so HR reviews every request individually.
Individual cases are handled by HR.

### HB-4.4 Equipment and home setup

The company provides a laptop, a monitor, a keyboard, a mouse and a headset for remote work.
You can claim up to BC 300 once every 3 years for a desk chair or other home office equipment.
Company equipment remains company property and must be returned when you leave.

## HB-5 Expenses and travel

### HB-5.1 What can be claimed

You can claim reasonable costs that you pay for company business, such as travel, accommodation, meals while travelling and approved training.
You cannot claim personal costs, fines, alcohol outside approved client events, or travel between your home and your usual office.

### HB-5.2 Limits

All amounts in this handbook are in the company's base currency, written as BC.
The meal allowance while travelling for business is up to BC 50 per day.
Hotel stays are limited to BC 180 per night unless your manager agrees a higher amount before booking.
Book rail or economy-class air travel for business trips.

### HB-5.3 Claim deadline

Submit an expense claim within 60 days of the date you paid.
Claims older than 60 days need written agreement from your manager and the finance team.

### HB-5.4 Receipts and approval

Every expense claim needs an itemised receipt.
Your manager reviews and approves expense claims for their team.
Expense claims over BC 1,000 also need review by the finance team at finance@widgets-demo.example.

## HB-6 Code of conduct

### HB-6.1 What we expect

Treat colleagues, customers and partners with respect.
Be honest in your work, your records and your expense claims.
Follow the law and company policies wherever you work.

### HB-6.2 Conflicts of interest

Tell your manager and the People Team about any outside job, investment or relationship that could affect your work decisions.
Do not take part in a decision about hiring, buying or pay that involves a close friend or family member.

### HB-6.3 Gifts and hospitality

You can accept a gift or hospitality worth up to BC 75 from a customer or supplier.
Report any gift or hospitality worth more than BC 75 to your manager before accepting it.
Never accept cash or a cash equivalent such as a gift card.

### HB-6.4 Confidentiality and social media

Keep company, customer and colleague information confidential, during and after your employment.
Do not post confidential company information on social media.
When you post about the company in a personal capacity, make clear that the views are your own.

## HB-7 Harassment and discrimination

### HB-7.1 What counts

Harassment is unwanted behaviour that violates someone's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile or offensive environment.
Discrimination is treating someone less favourably because of a personal characteristic such as age, disability, gender, race, religion or sexual orientation.
Harassment and discrimination are not tolerated at work, at work events or in online work spaces.

### HB-7.2 How to report

You can report harassment or discrimination to your manager, to any member of the People Team, or anonymously through the Speak Up form at https://speakup.widgets-demo.example.
If the concern involves your manager, report it to the People Team or through the Speak Up form.
You can report something that happened to you or something you saw happen to someone else.

### HB-7.3 Confidentiality and no retaliation

Reports are shared only with the people who need to know in order to look into them.
No one may be treated badly for raising a concern in good faith or for helping with an investigation.
Retaliation against a person who reports a concern is a serious conduct matter.

### HB-7.4 What happens after a report

The People Team acknowledges every report within 2 working days.
The People Team aims to complete an investigation within 30 days and keeps the person who reported informed.
The People Team can put temporary measures in place during an investigation, such as changing reporting lines or seating.
Individual cases are handled by HR.

## HB-8 Grievances and whistleblowing

### HB-8.1 Raising a grievance

A grievance is a formal complaint about your work, your working conditions or how you have been treated.
Try to resolve a concern informally with your manager first, where that feels safe.
To raise a formal grievance, email the People Team at people@widgets-demo.example.
You can bring a colleague with you to any grievance meeting.

### HB-8.2 Reporting wrongdoing

Report suspected fraud, bribery, safety risks, data misuse or other wrongdoing through the Speak Up form at https://speakup.widgets-demo.example.
Speak Up reports go to the Ethics Committee, which is independent of your management line.

### HB-8.3 Protection for people who report

People who report wrongdoing in good faith are protected from retaliation, even if the concern turns out to be unfounded.
Individual cases are handled by HR and the Ethics Committee.

## HB-9 Health, wellbeing and workplace adjustments

### HB-9.1 Wellbeing support

Every employee and their household can use the confidential Wellbeing Line for up to 6 free counselling sessions per leave year.
Contact the Wellbeing Line at wellbeing@widgets-demo.example.
The company is not told who uses the Wellbeing Line.

### HB-9.2 Workplace adjustments

If a disability or a health condition affects your work, you can ask for a workplace adjustment.
Examples of adjustments include changed working hours, specialist equipment or a different place of work.
Ask the People Team for an adjustment; you do not need to tell your manager the details of your condition.

### HB-9.3 Medical information

Medical information is held by the People Team only and is not shared with your manager without your consent.
Individual cases are handled by HR in confidence.

## HB-10 Benefits overview

### HB-10.1 Health cover

Every employee is covered by the company health plan from their first working day.
The company health plan can also cover a partner and dependent children.

### HB-10.2 Retirement savings

The company matches employee retirement savings contributions up to 5 percent of base salary.
You can join the retirement savings plan at any time.

### HB-10.3 Learning budget

Every employee has a learning budget of BC 1,000 per leave year for courses, books and conferences.
Agree how you use your learning budget with your manager before booking.
Unused learning budget does not carry over to the next leave year.

### HB-10.4 Pay and compensation questions

Questions about your salary, bonus, pay review or payslip go to the People Team at people@widgets-demo.example.
Pay is reviewed once a year in April.
Pay changes are agreed by HR and your manager and are never made through the HR assistant.
Individual pay questions are handled by HR in confidence.

## HB-11 IT and information security

### HB-11.1 Accounts, passwords and multi-factor authentication

Use a password of at least 14 characters for your company account.
Multi-factor authentication is required on every company account.
Never share your password or a sign-in code with anyone, including the IT Service Desk.

### HB-11.2 Devices

Lock your screen whenever you leave your device unattended.
Company devices lock automatically after 5 minutes without activity.
Report a lost or stolen device to the IT Service Desk at it-help@widgets-demo.example within 24 hours.
Only install software from the company software catalogue.

### HB-11.3 Data handling and AI tools

Store company data only in approved company systems.
Do not paste confidential company data or personal data about colleagues or customers into AI tools that the IT Service Desk has not approved.
Personal data about colleagues is only used for the purpose it was collected for.

### HB-11.4 Phishing and security incidents

Report a suspicious email with the Report Phishing button in your mail app, or forward it to security@widgets-demo.example.
Report any suspected security incident to security@widgets-demo.example straight away, even if you are not sure.
You will never be blamed for reporting a mistake such as clicking a suspicious link.

## HB-12 Onboarding

### HB-12.1 Before your first day

You receive your start date, your office location and the name of your manager by email before your first day.
Your laptop and accounts are prepared by the IT Service Desk before your first day.

### HB-12.2 The onboarding checklist

Every new joiner gets an onboarding checklist based on the template for their role.
Tasks on the onboarding checklist are owned by IT, admin, your manager or your buddy.
Any change to the onboarding checklist template for a new joiner is agreed by their manager.

### HB-12.3 Your first 30 days

Every new joiner is given a buddy from another team for their first 30 days.
Your manager meets you at least once a week during your first 30 days.
Complete the mandatory security and code of conduct training within your first 30 days.

### HB-12.4 Probation

The probation period is 90 days from your start date.
Your manager reviews your probation with you before the end of the 90 days.
The probation period can be extended once, by up to 90 days, after a discussion with HR.

## HB-13 Performance and conduct concerns

### HB-13.1 Regular check-ins

You meet your manager for a one-to-one check-in at least every 2 weeks.
A written performance review happens twice a year, in April and October.

### HB-13.2 Performance improvement plans

If performance falls below what your role needs, your manager discusses it with you first.
A performance improvement plan sets clear goals and support over a period of 6 to 12 weeks.
HR is involved in every performance improvement plan.

### HB-13.3 Disciplinary process

The disciplinary process is led by HR.
The stages of the disciplinary process are an informal discussion, a written warning, a final written warning and dismissal.
Serious misconduct can skip stages of the disciplinary process.
You can bring a colleague with you to any disciplinary meeting.
Individual cases are handled by HR.

## HB-14 Leaving the company

### HB-14.1 Resignation and notice

To resign, email your manager and the People Team.
The notice period after probation is 4 weeks.
The notice period during probation is 1 week.

### HB-14.2 Leave balance when you leave

Plan any remaining annual leave with your manager before your last day.
HR confirms how unused or overused annual leave is settled in your final pay.

### HB-14.3 Returning equipment and access

Return your laptop, badge and other company equipment on or before your last day.
Your company accounts are closed at the end of your last working day.

### HB-14.4 Exit interview

Every leaver is offered an exit interview with the People Team.
Exit interview feedback is shared only in summary form, without your name.

### HB-14.5 Dismissals and redundancies

Dismissals and redundancies are handled by HR only.
Managers do not dismiss employees without HR.
If you have a question about a possible dismissal or redundancy, contact the People Team directly at people@widgets-demo.example.
Individual cases are handled by HR in confidence.
